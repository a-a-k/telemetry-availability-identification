# G0: explicit AINA fixed-count adaptation to ordinary calibration

Version `G0-AINA-fixed-count-ordinary-v1` preserves the fixed-count, uniformly selected replica-failure subset and all-required graph-reachability calculation of the retained AINA source (`a-a-k/otel-demo-resilience`, commit `a8bc5a29a15443ad3a4ef6b88ac710afb97c70c0`, `scripts/resilience.py`, draw_alive_fixed/endpoint_success). [Exact historical replay](milestones/ORIGINAL_AINA_FULL_AUDIT.md) remains the evidence for the published algorithm. This new ordinary-input adaptation is explicitly a different parameterization, pool/operation binding and numerical implementation; it is not a rerun of the original fault scenario.

The adapter uses the same native-derived G/R and declared external-operation required nodes as the new graph method. Every eligible replica has one ordinary request-aligned eligibility coordinate. Non-target dependencies are explicitly excluded from the failure pool. Communication/domain effects may affect the observed target eligibility, but separate physical communication/domain probabilities are not recovered. Compound primitive gates/aliased replica coordinates are unsupported in this adapter. No controller state, true injection rate, business success label, call-completion coordinate, demand coordinate or deadline enters p.

For N eligible replicas and n calibration attempts, let f count known-failed slots and m masked slots, with the denominator N*n including all attempted slots. The sharp empirical mean-failed-fraction interval is [f/(N*n),(f+m)/(N*n)]. Complete observations identify the mean p. This does not identify a uniform fixed-count state law: that law is the predecessor model's retained assumption. For each possible p, k follows the original Python float conversion/round rule, including minimum1 when p>0 but rounding gives0; k is clamped to [0,N]. Because the rounding map is monotone, all possible k lie from k(p_lower) to k(p_upper). For N<=16 all intermediate integer counts are attained. The adapter enumerates each uniform k-subset exactly and evaluates the source all-block/all_of reachability predicate. It returns the minimum/maximum probability over possible k, with no point if these differ. If the mean remains ambiguous but every possible k gives the same target probability, the target is reported as singleton without claiming point identification of p.

The original scenario p becomes the calibrated mean proxy-ineligibility fraction, and the original broad killable container pool becomes the explicitly declared measured replica pool of the new design. These adaptations are necessary to avoid passing the new experiment's generating truth to the predecessor. If the operation has no eligible target replica, it receives a deterministic no-failure-pool graph calculation; the old source's empty-allowed-list fallback to killing unrelated services is not used. The new whole-operation required nodes come from the same declared source/contract mapping; static reachability forecasts the stricter business event only under ideal execution assumptions. AINA's historical entry/target definitions and its five scenario fractions are reported separately.

Exact enumeration replaces Monte Carlo in this small pool; simulation error is zero. This numerical change evaluates the same finite uniform-subset probability, not a better-fitted state law. No independent-marginal fit or freely tuned residual is introduced. Seven bounded controls pass, including direct execution of the retained source functions with every relevant artificial subset, positive/zero p rounding, no-eligible-pool behavior, graph mutation, discarded completion/demand/timing columns, masked mean bounds and target identification despite ambiguous p.

A controlled distinction is essential: a law with both replicas down on30% of attempts and both up on70%, and a law with only a down on30%, only b down on30%, both up on40%, share mean p=.3. Their true static graph probabilities are .7 and1. G0 maps both to uniform k=1 and predicts1; G-ID's joint observation law distinguishes them. This establishes what information the new identification step preserves under the declared known-control law; it does not promise improvement on applications or turn estimated proxy states into physical states.

Source-only placement transfer remains unsupported without a separately identified changed eligibility law. Quantization in p is disclosed; a smooth response to p is not promised. Costs of shared graph extraction are charged once and attributed to every consumer; a model-derived technical G0 replay does not make native acquisition/extraction free or establish end-to-end G0 isolation on a future main run.
